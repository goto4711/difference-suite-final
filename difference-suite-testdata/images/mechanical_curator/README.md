# BL Mechanical Curator collection

30 public-domain illustrations from the **British Library Mechanical
Curator collection** — book illustrations extracted automatically from
17th–19th century works digitised by the BL and released to Flickr Commons
in 2013 with no known copyright restrictions.

## Provenance

- Source: <https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Images_from_the_British_Library_Mechanical_Curator_collection>
  (BL Flickr Commons release, rehosted on Wikimedia Commons)
- Rights: No known copyright restrictions on the BL originals. The Commons
  re-uploads carry the BL's own no-known-rights statement. Per-record
  license info is in `manifest.json` where Commons recorded it.
- Thumbnail width: 1024 px.

## Selection

Drawn across four BL Mechanical Curator subcategories so the visual
register varies:

- **Portraits** — engravings of historical figures, period faces. Tests
  whether CLIP and Florence-2 can read a 19th-century engraving as a
  portrait rather than as "an illustration" or "a drawing".
- **Maps** — historical cartography (York 1761, Java 1812, Iceland 1819,
  Helgoland 1826…). The captioner usually narrates these as "a drawing of
  a piece of paper" or similar.
- **Thematic collections** — varied book illustrations: travel writing,
  novels, civil war prints.
- **Ships & Boats** — period marine illustration.
- **Broad fallback** — any image from the parent collection, to fill out
  the count.

## Why this collection

This set is pedagogically more interesting than press photography because
the models have *less* training on 17th–19th century book illustration.
The failures are louder and the contestation surface is wider:

- **Visual Storyteller** captions a 1761 map of York as "a drawing of a
  piece of paper with writing on it". Students can contest the
  representational reduction.
- **Imagination Inspector** can be run alongside ("imagine an engraved
  portrait") to compare its synthetic output against a real BL engraving.
- **Glitch Detector** trained on a "normal" set of 19th-century engravings
  will flag the maps as anomalies — a useful object lesson in what
  "normal" means in a training set.

## Manifest

Same shape as the Anefo / Rijksmuseum collections: filename, Commons title
and URL, descriptive title, description, date (where available), artist,
credit, license, license URL, byte size, source category.
